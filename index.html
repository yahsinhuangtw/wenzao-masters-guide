AI for SDGs · SDG 4 and SDG 10

Mandarin campus information, made something an international master's student can act on.

At Wenzao Ursuline University of Languages, most news, deadlines and rules are posted in Mandarin. We wrote a guide that any AI assistant can load, so you can ask in English and get an answer with the Mandarin terms next to it, or a clear "I don't have that, ask this office".

The problem

Notices are there. Students still miss them.

We asked international students at Wenzao how they hear about campus news and how well they can read it.

85.4%

had missed an official notice or opportunity at least once (41 of 48)

70.8%

usually hear about things through LINE group chats (34 of 48)

77.1%

need a lot of help to read Mandarin notices, or cannot read them (37 of 48)

46.4%

of translation-app users feel confident they understood a notice correctly (13 of 28)

Read these numbers with care: n = 48, exploratory convenience sample. Online survey, 1–7 October 2026. The 48 respondents were international undergraduate (58.3%), master's (29.2%) and exchange (12.5%) students, so this is not a master's-only sample. Answers are self-reported, and the survey did not record department or nationality. It shows a pattern, not exact rates for Wenzao.

Our focus · the guide

Give the AI a guide written for this job

The guide is a set of plain Markdown files. No app to install: you point your own AI assistant at it. It covers department and school news and events, choosing a thesis topic and advisor, library access, course selection, and the 2026–27 calendar.

1

Organize it in one place

Scattered pages, posts and rules are written up in one set of files, with the Mandarin term next to the English one (for example 校內公告, the school-wide announcement list) so you can recognise it on the real page. Includes bilingual email templates and an importable calendar file.

2

Give the AI its own instructions

SKILL.md tells the assistant how to behave: ask at most one thing first, answer first, keep it short, say where the information came from. llms.txt is an index of links, and llms-full.txt is everything in one file.

3

Say "I don't have that"

When the guide does not know something, such as a staff name or office hours, the assistant must say so and name the office to ask, instead of inventing an answer. Facts we have not confirmed with staff are marked as unverified.

The idea of a guide written for the AI, with a rule to say when nothing was found, was inspired by OpenFun's data platform for Taiwan public data. We applied it to one campus.

Try it

Paste one message into your AI assistant

Works with a chat assistant that can open web links. Copy this, paste it as your first message, then ask your question in English.

Read https://yahsinhuangtw.github.io/wenzao-masters-guide/llms-full.txt and follow it as your instructions. I'm a master's student at Wenzao.

  • If your assistant cannot open links: download llms-full.txt, attach it to the chat, and send the same message without the link.
  • If it says it cannot read the link: the first time a new link is opened it may be cached for a few minutes. Wait a little and try again, or use the download.
  • Calendar: wenzao-115-masters.ics has the key 2026–27 dates to import into your phone calendar. Dates come from the guide and are not yet confirmed by the offices.

Results, with limits

Our own tests: better than no guide, not proof

Share of checks passed in round 1 (25 test questions)
QuestionsWith the guideNo guide
All 2594%55%
News and events (6 new questions)94%45%
School-wide news (question 26, round 3)7 of 7 checks1 of 7 checks
What these numbers do not show
  • The testers were Claude subagents, each question run once, and the answers were graded by Claude. Differences of one check are noise.
  • Later rounds changed the guide and re-ran only 10 questions each; they stayed around 90–95%.
  • The survey behind the problem is small (n = 48) and not limited to master's students.
  • Many Wenzao facts in the guide are marked unverified or TODO(verify). No university office has checked them yet.
  • One real student tried the first version and found it too chatty and suggested a LINE bot. That led to the shorter start, the news module and the school-wide announcements. It is one person, not an evaluation.

What's next

Ideas, not promises

A LINE digest

Our survey and our first real tester both point to LINE. An idea is a short weekly summary of new notices in English, delivered where students already look. Not built yet.

Check facts with the offices

Ask the departments and school offices to confirm contacts, rules and dates, so that "unverified" labels can come off.

More departments

The guide currently has pages for two master's programs, GIES and DIA. Other departments would need their own pages.

Experiment: a map of Taiwan events from the school-wide announcement list (conferences, forums, workshops and lectures at other universities and agencies). Early prototype: fields were read from the posts on 2026-10-15 and not independently verified. Not part of the guide itself.

Updates

Test rounds so far

  1. 2026-10-15 · Round 3

    Guide v4: past theses mentioned in the first topic reply, no example topics in the hello, short lookups, at most five items listed. School-wide announcement list added. Result: 95% on the regression questions; the new school-wide news question went from 3 of 7 to 7 of 7 checks.

  2. 2026-10-15 · Round 2

    Guide v3: ask the department only when it matters, list the next dated must-not-miss items, shorter lookups. 90% vs 88% on 10 questions, within noise.

  3. 2026-10-15 · Round 1

    Guide v2 after a first real tester said it asked too much: one question at most, answer first, new news and events module. 94% vs 79% (previous version) vs 55% (no guide).

  4. 2026-10-15 · Round 0

    Baseline, 19 questions: guide 97% of checks, no guide 58%.
