# Project page outline (index.html)

Structure follows openfun.tw (hero, about, focus in three steps, works, updates, team, footer); look is our own. English only; Mandarin appears only inside real test excerpts and a few terms already in the repo. No 中/EN toggle (no reviewed translation).

1. **Hero** – "Mandarin campus information, made something an international master's student can act on." Buttons: Try it, Read llms.txt. Badge: pilot, not official.
2. **The problem** – survey (Oct 1–7 2026, n = 48 international students, exploratory convenience sample, 29% master's, not only master's): 85.4% had missed an official notice; 70.8% rely on group chats; 77.1% need a lot of help reading Mandarin notices; 46.4% of translation-app users feel confident they understood correctly. Caveat sits next to the numbers.
3. **Three ideas** – (1) one place, Mandarin next to English; (2) instructions written for the AI (SKILL.md, llms.txt); (3) "I don't have that, ask this office" instead of inventing.
4. **Try it** – copy-button prompt, fallback (download llms-full.txt and attach), cache note, .ics link.
5. **Examples** – four test excerpts (school-wide news, short hello, honest "I don't have", office phone), labelled as Claude subagent test answers, 2026-10-15.
6. **Results** – 25 questions: ~94% of checks passed with guide vs ~55% without; news/events 94% vs 45%; caveats: one run each, graded by Claude, small survey, Wenzao facts unverified. Link to iteration log.
7. **What's next** – LINE digest idea, verification with offices, more departments.
8. **Updates** – test rounds 0–3 (2026-10-15).
9. **Team** – placeholders only.
10. **Footer** – not official, pilot, repo, how to report a wrong fact (GitHub issue). Hidden video slot commented out.
