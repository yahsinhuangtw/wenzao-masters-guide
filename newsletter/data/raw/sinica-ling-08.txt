Abstract
Olsson (2013) and Dahl & Wälchli (2016) classify a number of morphemes as
IAMITIVES, a new grammatical category, which shares the resultative meaning with
the perfect, but is similar to already in having a change-of-state meaning.
In this talk, I question the typological validity of this new category, based on the
study of the syntax and semantics of Javanese wis (Indonesia), drawing from elicitationbased
fieldwork as well as corpus data. I argue, in joint work with Lisa Matthewson,
that wis is best analyzed as a focus operator with the semantics of already, similar to
Krifka (2000) (Vander Klok & Matthewson 2015).
I show new evidence from syntax and its corresponding semantic scope,
corroborating the analysis of Javanese wis as a focus marker already. For other
languages, the hypothesis is that the simplest analysis of markers that have these
change-of-state readings is that they either correspond to perfect or to already and that
cross-linguistic differences in between them can be derived from their interaction with
verbal aspect and from paradigmatic contrasts with other markers.
